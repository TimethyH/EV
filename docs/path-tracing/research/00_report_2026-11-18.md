# Trace in DXR, learn CUDA at the seams

Studios ship real-time path tracing as a **hybrid**. A raster G-buffer handles primary visibility, ReSTIR DI handles direct light, and **1–2 traced indirect bounces end in a world-space radiance cache** (SHaRC, or NRC in RTX Remix). One neural pass then denoises and upscales the result (DLSS Ray Reconstruction). "Reference" path tracers are a different thing: the UE Path Tracer, Unity HDRP and RTXPT's reference mode are separate progressive accumulators, denoised with OIDN or OptiX once enough samples have built up. Spectral hero-wavelength transport is still an offline-renderer feature (Weta's Manuka, pbrt-v4, Mitsuba 3). The plan below meets all of your constraints. EV gets an **engine-level, toggleable `PathTracer` module**. It traces with **DXR inline RayQuery in a render-graph compute pass** and carries **4 hero wavelengths in a `float4`**. Each camera ray's spectral result is converted to RGB, which goes to an **`IDenoiser` whose first backend is the OptiX AOV denoiser**, living in an optional CUDA DLL connected through shared D3D12 buffers and a shared fence. The ocean enters the acceleration structure as a compute-displaced vertex buffer with a refit BLAS. Reference mode freezes ocean time so accumulation can converge. CUDA goes where it teaches you the most without breaking the AMD PC: the denoiser, the interop kernels, offline spectral tables, and NRC training. It does not do the tracing, because that would make the renderer NVIDIA-only. For NRC, the route is **SHaRC first** (it runs on both PCs), then NVIDIA's NRC SDK as ground truth, then your own CUDA network (tiny-cuda-nn first, then hand-written), and finally HLSL `dx::linalg` once Shader Model 6.10 goes retail (expected in Agility SDK 1.621, early December 2026). The first three milestones are plumbing. EV has no DXR code today, its descriptor heaps are per-command-list (1024 entries, not bindless), its render graph records one command list per frame, and the ocean is displaced only in the vertex shader. The 18 decisions below are yours to make. Each one comes with a recommended default.

## Shipped games trace hybrid paths; reference modes accumulate apart

### The production recipe in 2026

Every shipped AAA "full ray tracing" mode so far is an NVIDIA-partnered PC mode, and they are built from the same toolkit. **Indiana Jones and the Great Circle** has the clearest public breakdown. At 4K output with DLSS-RR Performance, path tracing runs at **1080p** on an RTX 5080, over a 26.9M-triangle acceleration structure. The `TraceMain` pass traces **up to 2 bounces and reuses SHaRC**, a separate `SharcUpdate` pass traces **up to 4 bounces** to keep the cache fresh, and `SunTracing` handles sun shadows. Opacity Micromaps cut TraceMain from **7.90 to 3.58 ms (−55%)**. Compacting dynamic vegetation BLASes cut BLAS memory from **1027 to 606 MB**, which let full path tracing run on 12 GB GPUs ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/path-tracing-optimizations-in-indiana-jones-opacity-micromaps-and-compaction-of-dynamic-blass)). The same article shows that productions mix APIs. Main paths use `traceRay` from ray-generation shaders, with any-hit shaders for alpha, while shadow rays use ray queries because some rays are launched from Forward+ fragment shaders.

id Software's Billy Khan says DOOM: The Dark Ages' path tracer took **about 6 months**. Its "biggest optimizations" came from Shader Execution Reordering (SER), and **BLAS refits "can be up to 10x faster" than rebuilds**. He also lists shading as one of the largest costs and advises "start with general ray tracing before path tracing" ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-id-software-used-neural-rendering-and-path-tracing-in-doom-the-dark-ages)). At GDC 2023, Cyberpunk 2077's Overdrive mode was described as RTXDI for all direct light with path tracing for the indirect part ([TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/306589/cd-projekt-red-and-nvidia-talk-about-upcoming-cyberpunk-2077-path-tracing)). Its default bounce and sample counts have never been published; the "2 rays, 2 bounces" figures come from a community mod ([AdvancedPathTracingCP2077](https://github.com/codecrafting-io/AdvancedPathTracingCP2077)). Half-Life 2 RTX (March 18, 2025) shipped **NRC as NVIDIA's first public neural shader**, which NVIDIA claims is "up to 15% faster" for indirect light ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/rtx-remix-half-life-2-rtx-demo-launching-march-18/)).

Denoising has collapsed into one learned pass. **DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction** (early access August 25, 2026) "unifies denoising and Super Resolution into a single model" and runs on all RTX GPUs ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/gamescom-2026-dlss-4-5-ray-reconstruction-release-announcements-trailers/)). The cross-vendor fallback is **NRD v4.18.0**. Its ReBLUR costs 2.55 ms and ReLAX 3.25 ms at 1440p on an RTX 4080 ([NRD](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/NRD)). AMD's **FSR Ray Regeneration 1.2.1** is RDNA 4-only and costs 3.74 ms at 1080p on an RX 9070 XT for 4 signals ([GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/amd-fsr-rayregeneration/)).

### Reference renderers are a separate mode, and that is what EV should build first

Reference path tracers follow a different pattern. The **UE Path Tracer** is its own viewport mode. It accumulates samples and applies Intel Open Image Denoise (by default) to the final sample ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/path-tracer-in-unreal-engine)). **Unity HDRP** accumulates up to a maximum sample count and restarts when the camera moves ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@13.0/manual/Ray-Tracing-Path-Tracing.html)). **NVIDIA RTXPT v1.9.0** is the closest open DX12 reference for your project. It has no rasterization at all, offers both a reference and a real-time mode (the reference photo mode uses the OptiX denoiser), and combines NEE-AT light guiding, Owen-scrambled sampling, RTXDI, SHaRC on by default, NRD and DLSS-RR. It requires DXR 1.1 minimum, SM 6.6 and Resource Binding Tier 3, and **does not officially support AMD**: its known issues include black transparencies on some AMD systems ([RTXPT](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXPT)). AMD's counterpart is **Capsaicin**, a D3D12 research framework with a reference path tracer and GI-1.2 radiance caching ([Capsaicin](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/Capsaicin)).

### Offline renderers show where spectral rendering pays off

Spectral transport is established offline. **pbrt-v4** uses `NSpectrumSamples = 4` over **360–830 nm** ([pbr-book 4.5](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Representing_Spectral_Distributions)). **Mitsuba 3** ships `*_spectral` variants, and its `cuda` backend JIT-compiles kernels and hands ray tracing to OptiX ([Mitsuba 3](https://mitsuba.readthedocs.io/en/stable/src/key_topics/variants.html)). Hero wavelength sampling itself comes from **Weta Digital**, whose Manuka traces "a continuously sampled wavelength … with each path" ([Weta FX](https://www-ext.wetafx.co.nz/research-and-tech/technology/physically-based-shading); [Wilkie EGSR'14](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/~wilkie/Website/EGSR_14.html)). Cycles stays RGB apart from spectral random-walk SSS ([ArchiVinci](https://www.archivinci.com/blogs/spectral-rendering)). The GPU cost is small. On an RTX 5070 Ti at 1080p and 1 spp, 4-wavelength spectral rendering adds **under 0.3 ms**: Bistro at path length 8 goes from **14.3 to 14.6 ms** ([Moments in Graphics](https://momentsingraphics.de/SpectralRendering2Rendering.html)). The notes infer that studios stay RGB because of asset pipelines, compositing and RGB-trained denoisers rather than GPU cost. They also flag the spectral status of RenderMan XPU, V-Ray, Corona, Hyperion and Arnold as unconfirmed.

## Six ideas to understand before writing a line

### Path tracing is Monte Carlo integration of one recursive equation

The rendering equation says that the light leaving a point is its emission plus all incoming light, weighted by the BSDF and the cosine term: `L_o(x,ω_o) = L_e(x,ω_o) + ∫_Ω f(x,ω_i,ω_o) · L_i(x,ω_i) · |cos θ_i| dω_i`. `L_i` is itself the `L_o` of whatever surface the ray from `x` hits, so the integral recurses. A path tracer estimates it by sampling one direction `ω_i` with pdf `p(ω_i)` and using `f · L_i · |cos θ| / p` as an unbiased estimate. It then repeats this at the next hit, multiplying a running **throughput** `β *= f·|cos θ|/p`. Variance falls as 1/N in the number of samples, so error falls as 1/√N. Quadrupling the spp halves the noise.

Three techniques make this practical. **Importance sampling** chooses `p` to resemble the integrand. For GGX, that means sampling the distribution of visible normals (Heitz 2018, JCGT; the notes list it but did not re-fetch it). **Next-event estimation (NEE)** connects every vertex directly to a light with a shadow ray, so small bright lights such as the sun get found instead of being hit by chance. **Multiple importance sampling (MIS)** combines NEE and BSDF sampling. Each strategy's contribution gets weight `w_i = p_i^β / Σ_k p_k^β` (balance heuristic for β = 1, power heuristic for β = 2), which removes the fireflies that either strategy alone produces on glossy surfaces near small lights. **Russian roulette** ends a path with probability `1−q` and divides surviving throughput by `q`. This keeps the estimator unbiased while bounding path length. With spectral throughput, use a scalar such as `max(β)` for `q`, the role pbrt's `MaxComponentValue()` / `Average()` serve ([pbr-book 4.5](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Representing_Spectral_Distributions)). **pbr-book 4th edition** is the primary textbook: free online, with source that matches the text. Veach's 1997 thesis is the canonical MIS reference, but the notes mark its URL as unverified.

The notes add one practical point about samplers. pbrt-v4 provides stratified, Hammersley and Sobol/Owen generators ([pbrt-v4 sampling.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mmp/pbrt-v4/master/src/pbrt/util/sampling.h)), and RTXPT uses Owen-scrambled low-discrepancy sampling ([RTXPT](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXPT)). Wavelength needs **its own sample dimension**, drawn once per camera ray.

### Hero wavelength sampling fits exactly into a float4

A spectral renderer replaces each RGB triple with radiance at specific wavelengths. Tracing one wavelength per path causes heavy colour noise. **Hero Wavelength Spectral Sampling** (Wilkie, Nawaz, Droske, Weidlich, Hanika, CGF 33(4), EGSR 2014) samples one random **hero** wavelength per path, places C−1 more at equal spacing so that "all path wavelengths together always evenly cover the visible range", and **makes every directional sampling decision with the hero alone** ([Wilkie EGSR'14](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/~wilkie/Website/EGSR_14.html); [EG DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v33i4pp123-131)). The reference implementation is pbrt-v4's `SampledWavelengths`. `SampleUniform` sets `lambda[0] = Lerp(u, 360, 830)` and steps each next wavelength by `Δ = 470/4` nm, wrapping around at 830. Each pdf is `1/470`. `SampleVisible` rotates in primary-sample space instead (`u_j = frac(u + j/4)`, `λ_j = F⁻¹(u_j)`), which stratifies the wavelengths under an importance density centred near 538 nm ([pbr-book 4.5](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Representing_Spectral_Distributions)). Ported to HLSL (HLSL has no `atanh`, so it is spelled out with `log`), pbrt's exact constants become:

```hlsl
// Hero wavelength state: 4 wavelengths, 4 pdfs, 4 throughputs, 4 radiances.
struct SampledWavelengths { float4 lambda; float4 pdf; };

float SampleVisibleWavelength(float u) {           // pbrt-v4 sampling.h, ported
    float x = 0.85691062 - 1.82750197 * u;
    return 538.0 - 138.888889 * 0.5 * log((1.0 + x) / (1.0 - x)); // atanh(x)
}
float VisibleWavelengthPdf(float l) {
    if (l < 360.0 || l > 830.0) return 0.0;
    float c = cosh(0.0072 * (l - 538.0));
    return 0.0039398042 / (c * c);
}
SampledWavelengths SampleVisible(float u) {
    SampledWavelengths s;
    [unroll] for (int j = 0; j < 4; ++j) {
        float uj = frac(u + j / 4.0);               // rotate in u-space
        s.lambda[j] = SampleVisibleWavelength(uj);
        s.pdf[j]    = VisibleWavelengthPdf(s.lambda[j]);
    }
    return s;
}
```

Code sources: [pbrt-v4 sampling.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mmp/pbrt-v4/master/src/pbrt/util/sampling.h) and [pbr-book 4.5](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Representing_Spectral_Distributions).

The subtle part is **spectral MIS**. In principle each wavelength `λ_j` "could have" produced the same path, so the balance heuristic over the C wavelengths gives `⟨I⟩ = Σ_j f(x,λ_j) / Σ_k p(x|λ_k)`. The notes flag this as an **unverified** recollection of the paper, to be checked against its Sections 3–4. In practice the formula collapses. With RGB-upsampled albedos, GGX lobes and a constant IOR, the sampling pdf does not depend on wavelength, so all `p(x|λ_k)` are equal and the MIS weight becomes a plain average over the four lanes. Wavelength-dependent pdfs appear only with **dispersion, spectrally varying media (water's σ_t(λ)) or spectral SSS**. For dispersion, pbrt's `TerminateSecondary()` sets `pdf[1..3] = 0` and divides `pdf[0]` by 4, after which only the hero continues ([pbr-book 4.5](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Representing_Spectral_Distributions)). For water, the notes recommend using the hero's σ_t for distance sampling, with spectral MIS or delta/ratio tracking with a majorant.

### RGB assets become spectra through a sigmoid, and spectra become pixels through the CMFs

Your textures and material constants are RGB, so each one needs a plausible spectrum. **Jakob & Hanika 2019** is the standard method. Each colour maps to three coefficients, and the spectrum is `S(λ) = s(c₀λ² + c₁λ + c₂)` with `s(x) = ½ + x / (2√(1+x²))`. It is energy-conserving, stores as compactly as RGB, costs "as few as six floating-point instructions" per wavelength, and has zero round-trip error over sRGB ([RGL EPFL](https://rgl.epfl.ch/publications/Jakob2019Spectral); [rgb2spec](https://github.com/mitsuba-renderer/rgb2spec)). pbrt-v4 stores the coefficients in a `float[3][64][64][64][3]` table, indexed by the largest channel. Grey inputs skip the table. pbrt also distinguishes three spectrum kinds: **albedo** (rgb in [0,1]), **unbounded** (scaled by `2·max(rgb)`), and **illuminant**, which is multiplied by the colour space's white point (D65 for sRGB) so that a "white" light stays white ([pbr-book 4.6](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Color)). On the way back, `ToXYZ` is a Monte Carlo estimate: `X = mean_j( x̄(λ_j)·L(λ_j)/pdf(λ_j) ) / 106.856895`, and likewise for Y and Z, followed by the XYZ→RGB matrix of the target space. Wyman, Sloan and Shirley's analytic CMF fits (JCGT 2013) remove the need for CMF tables on the GPU ([JCGT](https://jcgt.org/published/0002/02/01/)).

The notes infer three GPU-specific points. The full table is about **9.0 MiB in fp32**; as three 64³ RGBA32F 3D textures it is about 4 MiB each. Its nonlinear z-remap (`zNodes`) has to be inverted or replaced with a 1D LUT before hardware trilinear filtering can be used. Precomputing per-texel coefficients offline cuts runtime cost to one fetch plus about 6 FLOPs per wavelength. In HLSL the sigmoid is `x = (c0*λ + c1)*λ + c2; s = 0.5 + 0.5*x*rsqrt(1 + x*x)`. Whether rgb2spec coefficients expect λ in nm or a normalized λ is **unverified** and needs checking in the repo. Peters' real-time alternative uses Fourier sRGB plus a **1024-entry RGBA16F LUT (8 KiB per illuminant)** that folds the CMFs, the illuminant and the XYZ→sRGB matrix together ([Moments in Graphics](https://momentsingraphics.de/SpectralRendering2Rendering.html)). The film buffer always stays RGB or XYZ, never spectral.

### Water is the strongest argument for going spectral

Pure water's absorption (Pope & Fry 1997) has a minimum of **0.0044 m⁻¹ at 418 nm** and rises about **100×** to **0.624 m⁻¹ at 700 nm** ([OMLC](https://omlc.org/spectra/water/abs/pope97.html); [data](https://omlc.org/spectra/water/data/pope97.dat)). Absorption sits in an exponent, `T = exp(−a·d)`, so over 10 m transmittance is about **94% at 440 nm, 58% at 550 nm, 11% at 600 nm and 1% at 680 nm**. An RGB "red" channel that averages 600–700 nm mixes 11% with 1%, and the exponential of an average is not the average of exponentials. RGB Beer–Lambert therefore gets the hue wrong as depth increases. This is derived in the notes, not taken from a water-specific publication. Molecular scattering is about **0.00288 m⁻¹ at 500 nm** with a **λ^−4.32** dependence (Morel 1974) ([DRDO review](https://publications.drdo.gov.in/ojs/index.php/dsj/article/download/6048/3176/15909)). Seawater's IOR depends on λ, temperature and salinity (Quan & Fry 1995, ten parameters) ([rho::n_water](https://rdrr.io/github/AlexCast/rho/man/n_water.html)). It varies by roughly 0.01 across the visible range, which matters for spray, rainbows and caustic fringes but is invisible on the bulk surface.

The surface is a dielectric interface, either smooth or GGX-rough as in Walter et al. 2007 (URL listed, not fetched). It gives **total internal reflection beyond about 48.6°** for rays inside the water ([Walter 2007](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~srm/publications/EGSR07-btdf.pdf)). Bruneton et al. 2010 treat sub-pixel waves as extra roughness, and that idea carries over to a path tracer: the traced mesh can be coarse, with slope variance below mesh resolution becoming GGX roughness ([HAL](https://hal.inria.fr/inria-00443630)). Seafloor caustics are **L-S-D-S-E paths**, which unidirectional NEE cannot connect, so they show up only when a BSDF-sampled path happens to hit the 0.5°-wide sun. The notes rank fixes by effort: roughen the interface for NEE, enlarge the sun, run a caustic photon pass (RTG II ch. 30), use MNEE, or use BDPT/MLT ([RTG II ch. 30](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/ray-tracing-gems/rtg2-chapter30-preprint.pdf)). EV's raster ocean fakes subsurface scattering with Garrett Gunnell's wave-height × view/sun term (`ocean_pixel.hlsl:258-261`). In a volumetric path tracer that glow appears on its own, so the reference mode can be used to **calibrate the fake**.

### The OptiX denoiser is just CUDA calls, and interop is buffers plus one fence

The OptiX denoiser is **driver-hosted** and needs only an `OptixDeviceContext`. No OptiX ray-tracing programs, pipelines or acceleration structures are involved, and you build against headers only ([optix-dev](https://github.com/NVIDIA/optix-dev)). **OptiX 9.1.0** (December 2025, on GitHub January 13, 2026) is the latest release. It requires an **R590+ driver and a Turing or newer GPU** ([optix-sdk releases](https://github.com/NVIDIA/optix-sdk/releases); [downloads](https://developer.nvidia.com/designworks/optix/download)). For new code, use **`OPTIX_DENOISER_MODEL_KIND_AOV`** for single frames and **`TEMPORAL_AOV`** for sequences. `UPSCALE2X` / `TEMPORAL_UPSCALE2X` denoise and upscale in one pass. LDR, HDR and TEMPORAL are legacy or deprecated ([optix_types.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/optix-dev/main/include/optix_types.h); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/optix-8-0-denoiser-camera-space-vs-world-space/262875)). The call sequence is:

1. `optixInit` and `optixDeviceContextCreate`.
2. `optixDenoiserCreate` with `guideAlbedo = guideNormal = 1`.
3. `optixDenoiserComputeMemoryResources(W, H)`, then allocate state and scratch.
4. `optixDenoiserSetup`, and again on every resize.
5. Per frame: `optixDenoiserComputeAverageColor` (AOV models), then `optixDenoiserInvoke` with the beauty layer first.

([optix_host.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/optix-dev/main/include/optix_host.h)). Temporal models also need flow vectors pointing **forward in time** (previous → current, the opposite of DLSS's convention), the previous output and two internal guide layers. Flow must never be NaN ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-is-optix-denoiser-comparing-nrd-and-dlss-which-one-is-best-for-offline-and-semi-realtime-denoise/357080)).

The **normal space is genuinely disputed**. NVIDIA staff recommend world-space normals on R535+ drivers, the `optixDenoiserInvoke` header comment still says camera-space for temporal models, and Falcor converts to camera space. Test both. NVIDIA staff also say OptiX works best around **16 spp**, while DLSS excels at very low counts, and some apps use DLSS while the camera moves and OptiX once it stops ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-is-optix-denoiser-comparing-nrd-and-dlss-which-one-is-best-for-offline-and-semi-realtime-denoise/357080)). Expect the **first call to take 1200–1500 ms** of initialization, so benchmark after warm-up ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/denoising-benchmarks-with-geforce-gtx-1080/66674)). No current millisecond figures at 1080p or 1440p were found. Measure them yourself.

Interop needs four pieces:

- **Shared memory.** Create committed buffers with **`D3D12_HEAP_FLAG_SHARED`**, export them with `ID3D12Device::CreateSharedHandle`, import them with `cudaImportExternalMemory` (type `D3D12Resource` plus the **required `cudaExternalMemoryDedicated` flag**), and map them with `cudaExternalMemoryGetMappedBuffer`.
- **Synchronization.** Use one **`D3D12_FENCE_FLAG_SHARED`** fence imported as `cudaExternalSemaphoreHandleTypeD3D12Fence`, with monotonically increasing values ([CUDA Runtime API](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-runtime-api/group__CUDART__EXTRES__INTEROP.html)).
- **Device matching.** Compare `cudaDeviceProp::luid` with the adapter LUID ([CUDA Programming Guide 13.5](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/graphics-interop.html)).
- **Linear buffers.** D3D12 textures use swizzled layouts and OptiX's `OptixImage2D` wants linear memory, so both Falcor and nvpro's `vk_denoise` **copy textures into shared linear buffers with compute shaders** ([Falcor](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Falcor/tree/master/Source/RenderPasses/OptixDenoiser); [vk_denoise](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_denoise)).

The per-frame ordering works out as follows:

1. D3D12 packs the buffers and calls `Signal(fence, ++v)`.
2. CUDA calls `cudaWaitExternalSemaphoresAsync(v)`, denoises, and calls `cudaSignalExternalSemaphoresAsync(++v)`.
3. D3D12 calls `Wait(fence, v)` and unpacks.

The CPU must issue the CUDA signal before submitting the D3D12 wait. Falcor's `getSharedDevicePtr()` never destroys its `cudaExternalMemory_t`, so do not copy that leak ([Falcor CudaUtils.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Falcor/master/Source/Falcor/Utils/CudaUtils.cpp)). The official sample is `cuda-samples/cpp/5_Domain_Specific/simpleD3D12`. Its exact fence sequence and size computation were not verified line by line ([cuda-samples](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cuda-samples/tree/master/cpp/5_Domain_Specific/simpleD3D12)).

### Neural radiance caching: short paths, self-training and a 64-wide MLP

Müller, Rousselle, Novák and Keller (SIGGRAPH 2021) trace a short path per pixel. A path stops once its area spread exceeds `c·a₀` (**c = 0.01**), and the rest of the light is read from a small MLP trained online. **Under 3%** of paths, one per tile, are extended by a few vertices. The cache is queried at their ends and the result propagated back as training targets. This **self-training** adds one bounce of transport per iteration. The network has **5 hidden layers of 64 neurons**, ReLU, no biases and half precision. Its inputs total 62 dims, padded to 64: 12-frequency position encoding, one-blob direction and normal, roughness, and albedos. Its output is multiplied by (diffuse + specular reflectance). Training takes **4 Adam steps × 16,384 records per frame** with relative-L2 loss, and the EMA α = 0.99. The cache converges in **about 8 frames**. Query plus training costs **1.59 + 1.11 ms on average at 1080p on an RTX 3090**, with a **13.6× speedup at equal error vs PT+ReSTIR** ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.12372v2)). NRC fails on caustics, contact shadows and glass ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.12372v2)), which matters for an ocean. Later NRC versions use Instant NGP's hash-grid encoding ([Instant NGP](https://nvlabs.github.io/instant-ngp/)). The Adam learning rate was not found in the paper text read. tiny-cuda-nn's default is 1e-3, and the notes suggest about 1e-2 only as a tuning start.

SHaRC is the non-neural sibling. It is a world-space **hash grid in plain HLSL** (SM 6.2 + fp16) with Update, Resolve and Query passes and **40 B per voxel, about 160 MiB at 2^22 entries**. Its update pass traces roughly one pixel per 5×5 block ([SHaRC Guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXGI/main/Docs/SharcGuide.md)). NVIDIA's NRC SDK has almost the same frame flow: update, query, QueryAndTrain, resolve ([NRC Guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXGI/main/Docs/NrcGuide.md)). That is why SHaRC makes a good stepping stone. No published work conditions a radiance cache on wavelength. The notes recommend caching RGB/XYZ first, because both SDKs and the paper are RGB, and they leave a wavelength-conditioned network as student research.

## EV has the bones of a path tracer but none of the DXR plumbing

EV (`T:\Projects\DX12\EV`, branch `render-graph`, HEAD `e738ba0`) started as Jeremiah van Oosten's LearningDirectX12 tutorial. An editor, hot reload, a render graph and crash reporting were added on top. A grep for `Raytracing|DXR|RayQuery|AccelerationStructure|…|D3D12SDKVersion` finds **nothing**. The pieces that help are real: the vendored `d3dx12` headers include `CD3DX12_STATE_OBJECT_DESC` and `CD3DX12FeatureSupport`; the render graph derives barriers from declared accesses; `GpuTimer` and the Render Graph panel time and capture any pass; and the shader library hot-reloads `cs_*` shaders compiled with DXC. The table lists what each part of the engine needs for path tracing (paths are relative to `EV-Engine/EV-Engine/`).

| Area | Today | What path tracing needs |
|---|---|---|
| Device (`source/core/application.cpp:501-562`) | `ID3D12Device13` at FL 11_0; Device13 fails on older Windows (the `Dads-Birthday` branch fell back to `Device2`, commit `5ce7993`) | Base device + optional `As<ID3D12Device5>`; gate on `OPTIONS5.RaytracingTier ≥ 1_1` |
| Feature checks (`application.cpp:428-438`) | Root signature 1.1 only | Caps struct: RT tier, shader model, binding tier, adapter LUID, vendor ID |
| Command lists (`command_list.h:93,511`) | `ID3D12GraphicsCommandList2`; `SetPipelineState` caches the last PSO (`command_list.cpp:1261-1274`) | `List4`; `DispatchRays`/`SetPipelineState1` wrappers must reset the PSO cache |
| Descriptors (`dynamic_descriptor_heap.*`, `root_signature.cpp:105-108`) | Per-list 1024-entry shader-visible heaps, mid-list heap switches, unbounded ranges unsupported | Short term: fixed staged table; long term: one persistent bindless heap |
| Resources (`resource.cpp:94-112`) | Always default heap, `HEAP_FLAG_NONE`, COMMON | AS path (RT-AS state, never transitioned), shared heaps for CUDA, a dedicated upload path for >2 MB (`upload_buffer.cpp:22-24`) |
| Render graph (`render_graph.*`, `docs/render-graph.md`) | One direct command list per frame, no async compute, no temporal resources, Tex2D-only transients | AS access kinds (UAV-barrier-only), a RayTracing/External pass kind, a frame split for CUDA, imported accumulation/history buffers |
| Shaders | `FxCompile` SM 6.0 per app; engine `.hlsl` not found by `ShaderLibrary` (`shader_library.cpp:24-50`); `ReadShaderTarget` doesn't know `lib_6_x` | Runtime DXC for engine shaders, or per-app lists; `lib_*` support only if DXR pipelines are used |
| Agility SDK | None, so the OS `d3d12.dll` is used | Needed for SM 6.6+ bindless and DXR 1.2 |
| Debug layer (`application.cpp:514-516`) | **Breaks on warnings** in Debug | Every DXR warning will stop the process, so keep validation clean |
| DRED (`application.cpp:1265-1290`) | No names for DXR ops | Add `DISPATCHRAYS`, `BUILDRAYTRACINGACCELERATIONSTRUCTURE`, … |
| Frame counter (`application.cpp:993`) | `m_frameCount++` commented out | Needed for deferred release in a persistent heap |
| Material (`header/resources/material.h`) | `MaterialProperties` is 140 B padded to 144 by `alignas(16)`; no transmission/clearcoat | A dedicated GPU material struct |
| Lights | App-side vectors; sun = `DirectionalLights[0]`, direction from a "position" | A path-tracer light list; env-map importance data |
| CI (`.github/workflows/windows-build.yml`) | WARP smoke test, no GPU, no CUDA toolkit | The feature must stay off on WARP; CUDA must be optional at build time |

The scene side maps cleanly onto acceleration structures. Every `Mesh` has one vertex buffer with a **60-byte stride** (position at offset 0, so `R32G32B32_FLOAT`, stride 60) and an **always-`R32_UINT`** index buffer, which gives one BLAS per mesh. A visitor like `SceneVisitor` can collect `(Mesh, GetWorldTransform())` pairs into TLAS instances each frame. No CPU copies of the geometry are kept, and no SRVs exist for the buffers yet.

The **ocean is the hard case**. `CommandList::CreatePlane` builds a static **513 × 513-vertex grid (524,288 triangles, 2 m spacing, 1024 m wide)**, and `ocean_vertex.hlsl:47-72` displaces it by summing four cascades with patch sizes **1500, 250, 17 and 5 m**. The vertex shader indexes the cascades by *object-space* xz while the pixel shader uses *world-space* xz (`ocean_pixel.hlsl:148-151`). These are identical only while the plane's transform is identity, and the editor gizmo can move it. Ocean time advances every frame, as does the animated point light (`ocean_scene.cpp:603`), so progressive accumulation never converges unless both are frozen. One cross-queue hazard is left over: parameter edits call `GenerateH0` on the **copy queue** without a GPU wait before the direct queue reads the cascades (`ocean_scene.cpp:961-971`). That needs fixing before a BLAS refit depends on those textures.

### D1 — Where the path tracer lives in the engine

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Inside OceanRenderer only** (`OceanRenderer/source/path_tracer.cpp`) | Fastest first pixel; direct access to cascade textures and `m_oceanPlane`; avoids the static-lib shader-path problem | Not reusable by other apps; the later refactor is guaranteed; misses the "core feature" goal |
| **B. Engine-core module** (`EV-Engine/header/raytracing/` + `source/raytracing/`, following the `snake_case` area convention) owning the AS manager, path-tracer passes, accumulation, `IDenoiser` and the editor panel; apps register *geometry providers* (the ocean's displaced VB + material ID + textures) and lights | Toggleable in every app; forces clean interfaces; matches how UE/Unity expose a path-tracer view mode | Needs D13 solved; more design up front; the engine must support app-defined "special" materials such as water |
| **C. Staged**: build in the app under `EV::PT` with files designed to move, then promote to B | Speed now, a path to B later | Ocean-specific assumptions leak in; the move may never happen |

**Recommendation: B.** Design it so that OceanRenderer is the only app wired up at first. The ocean provider is small: a displaced-position buffer, the static index buffer, a material ID, and the cascade textures plus patch sizes for the hit shader. ShaderArt is not a candidate yet because it is not on the render graph. A "Raster / Path traced" toggle in the viewport toolbar (`Editor::DrawViewportToolbar`, `editor.cpp:808-832`) needs a new editor render-mode callback because the toolbar is engine code. A "Path Tracing" panel through `Editor::AddPanel` works today. *Your call.*

### D2 — DXR pipeline vs inline RayQuery vs OptiX tracing

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Inline RayQuery (DXR 1.1) in a compute megakernel** | Fits a render-graph compute pass with no graph change beyond the AS access kind; **hot reload works today** (`cs_6_5`+ profile read from the `.cso`); no shader table or local root signatures, which fits EV's staged-descriptor model; AMD's RDNA guidance generally favours RayQuery (training knowledge, per notes); Indiana Jones uses it for shadow rays | All materials in one uber-shader, so more divergence; no hit-group material dispatch; alpha test is handled in the candidate loop; SER only via DXR 1.2 `HitObject` from RayQuery (the notes say to verify in the spec) |
| **B. DXR pipeline (`DispatchRays`)** | What RTXPT uses; `MaybeReorderThread` SER from raygen (reorders on RTX 40+, no-op on RX 9000) ([Microsoft](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/directx/shader-model-6-9-retail-and-more/)); per-material hit groups; any-hit alpha is natural | Needs `List4` wrappers and a PSO-cache reset; a new state-object + SBT class; `ReadShaderTarget` and entry-point changes for `lib_6_x` hot reload; local root arguments are awkward with per-list dynamic heaps, so use a global root signature only |
| **C. OptiX tracing in CUDA** | Deepest CUDA learning; mature OptiX samples; denoiser in the same API; easy tiny-cuda-nn integration | **NVIDIA-only, so the RX 9070 XT PC cannot run it**; two scene representations; output interop every frame; DLSS-RR, NRD and FSR need D3D12-side inputs; "using DLSS inside an OptiX application is difficult" ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-is-optix-denoiser-comparing-nrd-and-dlss-which-one-is-best-for-offline-and-semi-realtime-denoise/357080)) |
| **D. Raygen + HitObject + uber-shader material evaluation** | Middle path with SER and no per-material SBT | Same plumbing as B; DXR 1.2 / SM 6.9 required |

**Recommendation: A now, B or D later.** Write the path loop and material evaluation as tracing-API-agnostic `.hlsli` functions, so a raygen shader can call the same code after the migration. Measure on both GPUs before migrating, since the notes found no 2025–2026 RayQuery-vs-pipeline benchmarks for RDNA 4 or Ada/Blackwell. If you want CUDA tracing experience, treat C as a side-quest, for example a small OptiX viewer over the same glTF scene that you diff against EV's output. *Your call.*

### D3 — D3D12 runtime and shader-model baseline

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. OS `d3d12.dll`, SM 6.5** | No new dependency | SM 6.6 `ResourceDescriptorHeap[]` and DXR 1.2 depend on the OS version; no SER/OMM |
| **B. Agility SDK retail** (≥ **1.619.0**, SM 6.9/DXR 1.2; latest retail **1.620.2**, Nov 9 2026) + DXC **1.9.2602.16** (.17 in downloads) shipped next to the exe ([Agility](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/directx/directx12agility/); [SM 6.9 post](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/directx/shader-model-6-9-retail-and-more/)) | Same runtime on both PCs; SM 6.6 bindless; SER/OMM available; matches RTXPT's default DXR 1.2 path | `D3D12SDKVersion`/`D3D12SDKPath` exports and `D3D12Core.dll` per app; CI packaging; AMD needs Adrenalin 26.2.1+ and NVIDIA 595+ for SM 6.9 |

EV's `ShaderCompiler` already loads `dxcompiler.dll` from the exe folder first (`shader_compiler.cpp`), so shipping a newer DXC is cheap. The notes do not say which DXC version the installed Windows SDK carries. *Your call.*

### D4 — Descriptor and bindless strategy

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Global root signature + fixed-size staged SRV table** (existing `DynamicDescriptorHeap`, committed with the compute setters) | No core refactor; works today; the TLAS SRV binds through a table via a null-resource SRV (`shader_resource_view.cpp:16-23`, `command_list.cpp:1320-1336`); Sponza has ~70 textures, the helmet 5 | About 1000 descriptors per list; table size is fixed in the root signature; mid-list heap switches |
| **B. | **C. A separate PT-only bindless heap, swapped per pass** | Isolates the refactor | Only one CBV/SRV/UAV heap can be bound at a time, so it fights the dynamic heaps in the same list; fragile |

**Recommendation: A for milestones M2–M7, then B as its own milestone (M8a)** before real-time mode and NRC. *Your call.*

### D5 — Render-graph integration and the CUDA submission split

The graph records everything on one direct command list (`docs/render-graph.md:96`), but a CUDA pass needs the GPU work before it to be *submitted* and signalled first.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. `.External()` pass flag**: `Execute` closes and submits the list so far, transitions shared resources to COMMON, signals the shared fence, runs the CUDA callback (`cudaWait…`/`cudaSignal…` on the stream), makes the queue `Wait`, and continues on a new list | One graph per frame; the panel shows the CUDA pass; reusable for NRC training; the tracker's "pending barriers at submit" design already copes with splits | `RenderGraph::Execute` must take a `CommandQueue`; `GpuTimer` can't time CUDA, so use `cudaEvent` timing; `CommandQueue` needs `Signal/Wait(fence, value)` helpers |
| **B. Two graphs around CUDA in `OnRender`** | No graph changes | The panel shows two graphs; per-frame handle invalidation; ad hoc |
| **C. Asynchronous, one-frame-late denoise** (`vk_denoise` style: denoise frame N while N+1 renders) | Hides denoiser latency; no CPU stall | Extra buffers; displayed image lags by a frame; suits reference mode only |
| **D. CUDA-in-Graphics (CIG)** context on a D3D12 queue (`cuCtxCreate` + `cigParams`, check `CU_DEVICE_ATTRIBUTE_D3D12_CIG_SUPPORTED`) ([CUDA Driver API](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-driver-api/cuda_driver_api/group__CUDA__CTX.html)) | Tighter scheduling; Nsight Systems 2026.4.1+ traces CIG streams ([release notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-systems/ReleaseNotes/)) | Driver API; **no official statement that the OptiX denoiser runs in a CIG context** |

**Recommendation: A**, with C as a later optimization for reference mode and D as an experiment. *Your call.*

### D6 — How the ocean enters the acceleration structure

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. "Ocean Displace Vertices" compute pass** after `Ocean Permute 3`, writing positions to a persistent UAV buffer, plus a BLAS built once with `ALLOW_UPDATE` and **refit (`PERFORM_UPDATE`) every frame**, with a periodic full rebuild | Cross-vendor DXR 1.1; a fixed-topology grid is exactly the "tree in wind" refit case; estimated **~0.5 ms refit / ~5 ms build / ~31–42 MB BVH at 512²** from NVIDIA's O(1G)/O(100M) primitives-per-second rule of thumb ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/rtx-best-practices/)); estimates only | Choppy displacement inflates node bounds, so rebuild every N frames or on wind changes |
| **B. Full `PREFER_FAST_BUILD` rebuild every frame** | Simplest; best tree each frame | About 10× the refit cost; NVIDIA's total AS budget guideline is "at most 2ms" ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/rtx-best-practices/)) |
| **C. AABB + intersection shader ray-marching the displacement** | No per-frame AS work | Much slower than hardware triangle tests; choppy displacement isn't a true heightfield |
| **D. Cluster templates (CLAS, DXR Part 2)** | Performance "comparable with traditional refit"; topology swaps for LOD ([DXR Spec Part 2](https://microsoft.github.io/DirectX-Specs/d3d/Raytracing2.html)) | Preview only (Agility 1.722, preview drivers); NVIDIA support for the preview not checked |
| **E. Tile instances of one periodic BLAS** to extend past 1024 m | Cheapest wide ocean | In EV the 1500 m cascade is larger than the plane, so copies show seams unless displacement is evaluated from world xz per copy |

**Recommendation: A.** Add one more trick. When reference mode freezes time, the ocean becomes static, so build it once with `PREFER_FAST_TRACE` for the best trace speed during accumulation. Keep normals and foam out of the BLAS. The hit shader should reconstruct the *undisplaced* grid xz from barycentrics and the primitive's vertex indices, then sample slopes and foam there, just as the raster pixel shader does. Fix the object-vs-world uv mismatch at the same time. Run the refit on the direct queue for now, since the graph has no async compute. NVIDIA notes that async compute "in many cases hides the cost almost completely" ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/rtx-best-practices/)), so it is a later option. *Your call.*

### D7 — Frozen time and accumulation

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Manual "Freeze ocean time" toggle** | Explicit | Easy to forget, which silently prevents convergence |
| **B. Reference mode auto-freezes** ocean time and the animated point light (manual override available) | Accumulation always converges; reproducible shots | Two notions of time in the app |
| **C. Never freeze; rely on temporal denoising or caches** | Honest real-time behaviour | Never converges; that only suits the real-time mode |

**Recommendation: B for reference mode, C for real-time mode.** Add a **scene-version counter** in the editor that increments on gizmo edits, reparenting, spawned scenes, Inspector material edits, `SetEnvironment`, ocean or light parameter edits and `ShaderLibrary::GetReloadCount` changes. Also compare view/projection matrices and react to `ConsumeViewportResize`. Each of these resets accumulation. *Your call.*

### D8 — Reference/accumulation mode vs real-time mode

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Reference only**: camera rays, progressive accumulation, OptiX AOV on the accumulated buffer | Simplest; ground truth for validating raster and materials; the OptiX denoiser's sweet spot (~16 spp+) | Not interactive while moving |
| **B. Real-time hybrid**: raster G-buffer for primaries, 1–2 bounces, a cache, a temporal denoiser | The shipped-game pattern | Needs motion vectors (the notes show no motion-vector pass in EV), D4-B, a temporal denoiser; low-roughness water at 1 spp is the denoisers' hardest case |
| **C. Both, sharing material and light code** | The reference validates the real-time mode, as in kajiya, RTXPT and UE | More code paths |

**Recommendation: C, reference first.** It is also the fastest first milestone. *Your call.*

### D9 — Wavelength count and sampling distribution

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **C = 4 in a `float4`** | pbrt-v4's default ([pbr-book](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Representing_Spectral_Distributions)); maps to HLSL/CUDA vectors; measured overhead < 0.3 ms at 1080p | Colour noise after dispersion termination |
| C = 8 (two `float4`s) | Less colour noise per path (inference) | Double the spectral ALU and registers |
| C = 1 (single wavelength) | Simplest | Heavy colour noise; this is the problem HWSS solves |
| **Distribution: uniform 360–830** | Trivial | Wastes samples where the CMFs are near zero |
| **Distribution: pbrt `SampleVisible`** | Closed form; already importance-sampled | Ignores the scene illuminant |
| Distribution: Peters `(|r̄|+|ḡ|+|b̄|)·i(λ)` via a 1024-entry LUT ([Moments in Graphics](https://momentsingraphics.de/SpectralRendering2Rendering.html)) | Lowest colour noise for one dominant illuminant | Multiple illuminants force merging into one power-weighted spectrum, which raises noise |

**Recommendation: C = 4 with pbrt `SampleVisible`, one new hero per camera ray.** Try the Peters LUT later as an experiment once the sun and sky have spectra (D17). *Your call.*

### D10 — RGB-to-spectrum method and colour pipeline

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Jakob–Hanika, runtime table** (three 64³ 3D textures, ~4 MiB each RGBA32F) | No asset pipeline; works for constants and textures alike | Requires inverting the `zNodes` remap; three lookups plus selection per texel |
| **Jakob–Hanika, precomputed per-texel coefficients** (RGBA16F) | One fetch + ~6 FLOPs per λ; "same storage as RGB textures" ([RGL](https://rgl.epfl.ch/publications/Jakob2019Spectral)) | Asset conversion step; filtering coefficients ≠ filtering RGB (standard practice anyway) |
| Peters Fourier sRGB | Real-time oriented; CMF and matrix folded into a LUT | Less documented outside his code |
| Smits 1999 / Otsu 2018 / Meng 2015 | Historic or data-driven alternatives ([pbr-book Further Reading](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Radiometry,_Spectra,_and_Color/Further_Reading)) | Not energy-conserving smooth sigmoids, or gamut-dependent |
| CMFs: Wyman 2013 analytic vs 1 nm CIE tables | Analytic needs ~10 lines and no textures ([JCGT](https://jcgt.org/published/0002/02/01/)) | Exact coefficients were not fetched in the notes |

**Recommendation: Jakob–Hanika.** Start with the runtime table and use pbrt's three spectrum kinds: albedo for base colour, unbounded for emission, illuminant × D65 for RGB lights. Move to precomputed coefficients later. The conversion is a good **first CUDA kernel**, cached to disk next to the texture the way assimp's `.assbin` cache works. Keep an HLSL fallback so the AMD PC can regenerate the cache. Accumulate linear Rec.709 RGB (via XYZ) and reuse EV's existing Tonemap pass. Its default "ACESFilmic" curve is actually Hable's Uncharted 2 curve (`sdr_pso.cpp:22-33`). *Your call.*

### D11 — Material model

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Port EV's raster metal-roughness GGX + Lambert** (`pixel.hlsl:238-335`) to a shared `.hlsli` BSDF with VNDF sampling | Same look in both renderers; the reference validates raster; how shipped PT modes work | No transmission or clearcoat in EV's import today |
| **B. Dedicated water material**: exact dielectric Fresnel, IOR n(λ) from Quan & Fry, rough GGX BTDF, Beer–Lambert medium from Pope & Fry, Morel scattering, stochastic Lambert foam layer weighted by the foam map | Physically meaningful spectral water; calibrates the raster SSS fake | Caustics stay noisy (L-S-D-S-E); dispersion costs colour noise |
| C. OpenPBR Surface 1.0 | Industry standard (training knowledge in the notes) | Offline-oriented; not used by shipped real-time PT |

**Recommendation: A + B.** Define a dedicated GPU material struct rather than reusing the 140/144-byte `MaterialProperties`. Give water a material ID. Turn dispersion on as an option that triggers `TerminateSecondary()`. Opacity materials need candidate handling in the RayQuery loop. *Your call.*

### D12 — Denoiser abstraction and which denoisers

| Backend | Runs on | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OptiX `AOV`** | RTX (Turing+, R590+) | Reference mode, accumulated buffer | Your stated requirement; albedo+normal guides; driver updates improve quality without a rebuild ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-is-optix-denoiser-comparing-nrd-and-dlss-which-one-is-best-for-offline-and-semi-realtime-denoise/357080)) |
| OptiX `TEMPORAL_AOV` | RTX | Moving camera at moderate spp | Forward flow; Blender testers report detail loss vs non-temporal mode and "a temporal memory of 1 frame" (community) ([devtalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/taking-a-look-at-optix-7-3-temporal-denoising-for-cycles/18656?page=2)) |
| **OIDN 2.5.1** | CUDA, **HIP (RDNA 4 since 2.3.3)**, CPU | AMD parity for reference mode | Imports D3D12 buffers and fences (semaphore API 2.5.0); RT filter "not temporally stable"; CPU devices can't import external memory ([OIDN](https://www.openimagedenoise.org/documentation.html); [releases](https://github.com/RenderKit/oidn/releases)) |
| NRD v4.18.0 | Any DX12 (HLSL; AMD by inference) | Real-time 1 spp | Needs motion, normal/roughness, view-Z, diffuse/specular split with hit distances ([NRD](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/NRD)) |
| Own SVGF / A-SVGF | Any | Learning | HPG 2017 / 2018 papers (URLs not fetched) |
| DLSS-RR (Streamline 2.14.16) | RTX | Real-time denoise + upscale | Needs app ID for release builds ([Streamline guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS_RR.md)) |
| FSR Ray Regeneration 1.2.1 | RX 9000 only | Real-time on the AMD PC | SM 6.6, Windows 11 ([GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/amd-fsr-rayregeneration/)) |
| Accumulate-only | Everything incl. WARP | Fallback, ground truth | — |

**Recommendation:** an `IDenoiser` interface (`Resize`, `Denoise(color, albedo, normal, flow?, …)`) with **OptiX AOV first**. Add **OIDN second**: it reuses the same shared-buffer and fence code and is the only OptiX-like option on the RX 9070 XT. Keep **accumulate-only** as the universal fallback. For real-time mode, pick NRD or your own SVGF. DLSS-RR and FSR Ray Regeneration become optional vendor extras. Feed albedo guides from the **RGB** albedo, not a single wavelength's reflectance, or the guide will flicker with λ. Denoise every N frames, or when the camera stops. *Your call.*

### D13 — Where shaders are compiled

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| A. Each app project lists the engine's `.hlsl` (`FxCompile`) | Uses the existing build | Every app must be edited; the Windows SDK's DXC version is fixed |
| **B. **Recommendation: B.** CUDA code is a separate matter: `.cu` files compile with nvcc through the **CUDA 13.5** Visual Studio build customization (`CUDA 13.5.props`; VS 2026 supported) inside the D14 DLL project ([CUDA install guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-installation-guide-microsoft-windows/index.html)). Choose nvcc architectures once you know the RTX model (compute capability 7.5, 8.6, 8.9 or 12.0; [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/cuda-gpus)). For in-shader NRC training later, consider Slang, which can emit DXIL and differentiate coop-vec types ([Khronos](https://www.khronos.org/developers/linkto/slang-in-action-slides)). *Your call.*

## Three machines, three capability sets

The exact RTX model is unknown, and it changes several answers, so identify it in M0 first. SER only reorders on **RTX 40xx+**, OMM is hardware-accelerated only there, nvcc architecture flags follow the compute capability (**7.5 RTX 20, 8.6 RTX 30, 8.9 RTX 40, 12.0 RTX 50**; [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/cuda-gpus)), and tiny-cuda-nn's FullyFusedMLP may need a narrower network or CutlassMLP on lower-end cards ([tiny-cuda-nn](https://github.com/NVlabs/tiny-cuda-nn)). ## Uncertainties carried over from the research

Several points in this plan rest on unverified or conflicting information. **The HWSS spectral-MIS estimator** and the paper's reasons for choosing C = 4 were not read from the PDF, which was larger than the fetch limit. **Whether rgb2spec coefficients assume λ in nm** is unconfirmed. The **Wyman CMF coefficients**, the **D65 and Planck code bodies**, and the **water IOR Cauchy fit** (A ≈ 1.32, B ≈ 0.003) were not fetched; use Quan & Fry's code instead. **Pope & Fry values above 700 nm** conflict: the paper covers 380–700 nm, the data file lists 720 nm, and NASA says 400–715 nm. **OptiX normal space** is contradictory across forum, header and Falcor. **OptiX denoise timings** at 1080p/1440p on current RTX cards and **D3D12↔CUDA fence round-trip costs** are unmeasured. Whether the **OptiX denoiser works in a CIG context** is undocumented. The **NRC SDK's driver floor (617.42)** comes from a search snippet only, and RTXGI changelog dates are missing. **NVIDIA public-driver support for `D3D12_LINEAR_ALGEBRA_TIER_1`** and **RDNA 4 training support in `dx::linalg`** are unverified. **SM 6.10 retail in Agility 1.621** is a Microsoft plan for early December 2026, not a fact yet. **NRD on AMD** and **SHaRC on AMD** are inferred from their being plain HLSL. **WARP DXR support** depends on the WARP version. The **ocean BLAS timings** are rule-of-thumb estimates, not measurements. Cyberpunk's and Alan Wake 2's bounce and spp defaults remain unpublished.

## Conclusion

The research shows that your four requirements pull in different directions, and the plan resolves each one by putting it at a clear boundary. Spectral transport lives *inside* the path loop as a `float4` and leaves it as RGB at the camera ray. That one decision lets OptiX, OIDN, NRD, SHaRC and NRC, all RGB-trained or RGB-keyed, work unchanged. CUDA lives *outside* the frame in a DLL, connected by a fence. That makes it a self-contained learning sandbox, and it means the AMD PC and the WARP CI never notice it is missing. The ocean, which makes EV unusual among path-tracing projects, is also the best argument for going spectral, because water absorption spans two orders of magnitude across the visible range. It is equally the best stress test of the acceleration-structure design, as a 524k-triangle deforming mesh refit every frame.

The NRC goal changes what "done" means for the early milestones. The real prerequisites for NRC are a persistent bindless heap, a render graph that can split its submission around a fence, and per-frame training records written from HLSL. A path tracer built only for reference accumulation could skip all three. Building the `.External()` pass for the OptiX denoiser (M6–M7) and moving to bindless before real-time mode (M8a) therefore pays off twice. The vendor-neutral endpoint, NRC trained in HLSL `dx::linalg` on both your GPUs, only becomes possible in December 2026. The CUDA route leading up to it is the long, educational way to arrive there knowing exactly what the network does.
